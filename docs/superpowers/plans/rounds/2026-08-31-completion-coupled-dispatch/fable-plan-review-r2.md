## 2. The design as a whole, fresh eyes

Shapes searched: kill before the claim; kill mid-body; kill in the exit-write-to-classify interval (the seam's interval); rerun of a completed wrapper; two concurrent first runs; missing tree at relocation; hung client; body self-exit; child tampering with the dispatch directory; receipt tampering; transcript forgery; caller misattribution across concurrent rounds; seam abuse by a hostile parent; classify tool failure. Most are closed by the plan's own tests and I will not restate them. Two findings:

**Finding A - the "receipt effectively immutable" claim is wider than its mechanism, and one uncovered field gates a classifier check.** The plan claims the wrapper-carried values make "the receipt effectively immutable without a second copy of it" (plan:262-269). The mechanism actually binds four fields: `dispatchDir` and `round` via state 5, `token` and `priorStateSha256` via state 6 (plan:334-337). The receipt has fourteen fields (plan:98-113). The other ten are editable after preparation with no detection. Two of them are read at classify time: `workdirEvidence` gates states 9-11 (plan:341-345), and `workingDirectory` feeds state 8. An edit of `workdirEvidence` to the literal `none` after preparation silently disables the working-directory confirmation for that round - the B5 check, the one carry-over the poll demanded be wired (plan:1979). The mirror-identity fields are inert to editing because the wrapper bakes them into its own text rather than re-reading the receipt (plan:140-149), which is exactly the right pattern - and its absence for `workdirEvidence` is the gap. The actor needed is a same-user writer during or after preparation, which is the residual-2 family (plan:1929-1932); but residual 2 is stated ONLY about the nonce, and the design prose states the opposite about the receipt. Under the repo's own F2 rule - a contract must describe the mechanism that actually holds (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-31-dispatch-invariants.md:266`) - either widen the mechanism (the cheap form: `-Prepare` hashes the finished receipt's bytes and writes ONE expected digest into the wrapper; `-Classify` re-hashes and compares, which covers every field and could replace the two-field compare) or narrow the claim and add this to the residual list. I searched for a non-deliberate path to the same effect and found none; this is honesty-plus-hardening, not an accident waiting.

**Finding B - child tampering inside the dispatch directory: searched, and the ordering defends it.** The child runs with write access to `$PSScriptRoot`. It could pre-write `exit` or `classification` - both are overwritten by the wrapper's `WriteAllText` AFTER the child returns (plan:174-175), so the tampering is clobbered. It could delete `claim` - state 7 fails the round, conservative (plan:338). It could rewrite the transcript's later lines - only the FIRST `workdir:` header counts (plan:376-379), and the header is emitted by the client before prompt content is processed. I found no false-success path through the child that does not require it to write a well-formed `reply`, which is the client's job anyway. The one child-reachable target OUTSIDE the wrapper's overwrite protection is the receipt - Finding A.

Beyond those: I searched for an unfinished, killed, hung or misattributed round reading as success and found none on the stated premises. The remaining premise is the one the plan itself names: that the harness reports a killed task's exit as non-zero, measured in Task 10 Step 2a with an explicit STOP if it fails (plan:1790-1811). That is the correct handling of an unprovable-in-advance premise.
