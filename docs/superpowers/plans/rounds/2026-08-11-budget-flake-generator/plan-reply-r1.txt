## Design questions

### Q1 — Budget thresholds

| Shape | Benefit | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Hard fail at 5000 | Simple and immediately enforceable | After the proposed move, likely leaves little margin and creates direct pressure to shave a load-bearing sentence instead of making a deliberate budget decision. |
| (ii) Warn at 5000, hard fail higher | Separates early signal from a true runaway stop | The warning can become permanent background noise again, and the file may consume the entire gap before anyone acts. |
| (iii) Hard fail at the exact landed size | Creates a strict ratchet | Every legitimate correction immediately requires either deletion or a ceiling edit; “stated reason” can decay into boilerplate. |

I recommend **(ii): retain 5000 as the soft signal and hard-fail at 5250**. The present 5404 body must still shrink, while a successfully moved body should clear the warning and retain roughly 250 tokens before the hard stop. The hard-error message should name relocation or a deliberate, reasoned ceiling raise as the two remedies. `evals/tools/skill_lint.py:249-254`, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-27-0150-backlog.md:1169-1184`.

The plan should require this release to finish below 5000, so the warning is not shipped already normalized. The known residual risk is that a future branch ignores the warning until 5250; that is preferable to a 5000 cliff incentivizing deletion of correctness text.

### Q2 — Harness rendering versus different evidence

Keep expectation 1. A genuine structured `tool_use` is the correct artifact for proving that the executor dispatched the command; the defect is that the harness destroys part of that artifact before grading it. The grader is explicitly forbidden to assume unstated work, while `compact_stream` says tool inputs are the evidence of actual calls. `evals/tools/run_behavioral_evals.py:115-129`, `evals/tools/run_behavioral_evals.py:370-375`.

Widening does not weaken the narration boundary if:

- only genuine structured `tool_use` blocks can produce the record;
- agent text continues through `_neutralize`;
- the record retains its tool-use id and tool name;
- JSON newlines remain escaped;
- the elision marker states the original length and omitted count;
- a prose event containing identical command text is tested and cannot enter the namespace.

`evals/tools/run_behavioral_evals.py:359-367`, `evals/tools/run_behavioral_evals.py:391-410`, `evals/multi-model-verify/test_multi_model_verify.py:1363-1386`.

I would use a bounded head-and-tail representation only with a fail-first maximum-path fixture proving all three dispatch tokens remain observable. If that cannot be guaranteed, render bounded windows around `codex exec`, `--sandbox read-only`, and the model flag directly from the original structured event, with the tool-use id and full-input hash. That remains tool evidence, not narration.

For residual measurement, **12 unchanged-tree runs** is defensible if authorized: at the recorded one-third pass rate, the probability of observing zero passes in 12 runs is under 1%. Predeclare the rule before running; do not reinterpret the sample afterward. The historical 2-of-6 control and 1-of-7 branch tally is at `execution-deviations.md:848-857`. No live behavioral runs were spent in this review.
